# Product

## Platform

web

## Users

The primary user is a private-markets client with their own book — someone who
already knows what a P/E ratio is and does not need it explained. They check in
two or three times a day rather than watching a tape all session: once before
the open to learn what moved overnight, once mid-session to check their own
exposure, once after the close. They read for long stretches on a large display,
and glance on a phone in between.

A secondary reader is the desk that serves them — the "concierge desk" and
"private execution desk" the interface already names. Their needs are the same
information at higher density.

## Product Purpose

Meridian is a private-markets terminal: quotes, fundamentals, technicals, peers
and holdings for a followed universe, plus an execution desk for acting on them.

Success is that a client can answer "what happened, and what does it mean for
me" without assembling it from five screens — and can act on the answer in the
same surface, without a context switch to a broker.

## Positioning

Meridian's difference is editorial rather than functional. Competing terminals
are optimised for the professional who is already staring at them all day and
therefore ship maximum density with no hierarchy. Meridian is optimised for the
reader who arrives cold, several times a day, and needs the interface to have
already done the ranking. That is why the interface is a ruled ledger rather
than a grid of cards: the structure itself asserts what is primary.

## Operating Context

- Read in a dim room on a large display for long stretches; glanced at on a
  phone between those stretches.
- Bound to market sessions: pre-market, open, closed, halted. The interface is
  read in all four, and what is true differs in each.
- The user arrives with a question already formed ("did NVDA hold?") more often
  than they arrive to browse.
- Instruments are the unit of navigation. The user thinks in tickers, and every
  surface should be reachable from one.

## Capabilities and Constraints

**Shipped today**
- A six-instrument followed universe with quotes, fundamentals, technicals,
  peer comparison and institutional holdings.
- A price chart (TradingView lightweight-charts) with six ranges, area and
  candle modes, and a volume pane.
- A simulated execution desk: market and limit order tickets, buy and sell,
  buying-power and position checks, fill and rejection notices.
- A local book: cash, positions, portfolio value.

**Constraints that bind future work**
- **The data layer is scaffolding for a live feed.** Everything is currently
  generated by a seeded LCG (`lib/market/series.ts`) pinned to a fixed `ANCHOR`
  timestamp so the server and the client agree byte-for-byte. Any new data must
  follow the same rule — a `Date.now()` or an unseeded random anywhere in the
  render path is a hydration failure.
- **Because a live feed is the destination, the design must reserve room for
  the states a feed produces**, even while the data is synthetic:
  - quote staleness and disconnection,
  - session state — pre-market, open, closed, halted,
  - order rejection and partial fill,
  - a regulatory disclosure surface.
  A layout that only looks correct when everything is live and green is not
  finished.
- Fixed `en-US` formatting everywhere, for the same hydration reason.
- No network dependency at runtime; the terminal must render fully offline.

**Terminology**
Instrument (not "stock"), book (not "account"), the desk (not "trading"),
the wire (not "feed"), the session (not "the market being open").

## Brand Commitments

- The name **Meridian**, set in Instrument Serif, with the eyebrow
  "PRIVATE MARKETS".
- The visual world recorded in `DESIGN.md` — a hand-ruled ledger, champagne
  gilt on warm black. This is established and is inherited by new surfaces, not
  reopened.
- Voice: declarative and unhedged. The interface states what is true and does
  not editorialise. Labels are nouns. No exclamation, no encouragement, no
  gamification.

## Evidence on Hand

- `lib/market/instruments.ts` — six fully authored fictional instruments
  (AAPL, TSLA, NVDA, AMZN, SPOT, MSFT) with prices, ratios, revenue histories,
  peers, holders, insights and news. **This data is synthetic.** It is
  demonstration material and must be labelled as such wherever a reader could
  mistake it for a real quote.
- `DESIGN.md` — the recorded visual system, including measured contrast
  corrections.
- No real feed, no broker relationship, no customer, no benchmark, no pricing.
  None of these may be invented.

## Product Principles

1. **The interface does the ranking.** The user should not have to scan to find
   what changed. Order is an editorial act, and Meridian performs it.
2. **State before detail.** Every surface answers "what is true right now"
   before it offers anything to explore.
3. **Structure by rule, never by card.** Hierarchy comes from lines, space and
   type — the ledger idiom — because a page of filled panels flattens
   importance into sameness.
4. **Density without noise.** High information per square inch is correct for
   this reader; decoration that does not carry information is not.
5. **Design for the ugly state.** Disconnected, halted, empty, rejected, and
   after-hours are the states that reveal whether a layout was designed or
   merely arranged.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG AA is the floor and is already measured in `DESIGN.md`: text bottoms out
  at `ink-4` (5.1:1); `gold-deep` (4.1:1) and gridline tones are decoration
  only and may never carry text.
- Direction is never carried by colour alone — sage and terracotta always pair
  with a sign, a glyph or a word.
- 44px minimum touch targets; a visible 1px gold focus ring at 3px offset on
  every interactive element.
- The price canvas is invisible to assistive technology and ships with a
  spoken summary and a visually-hidden data table of the sampled series. Any
  new canvas or SVG chart inherits that requirement.
- Every motion path has a `prefers-reduced-motion` branch.
